## 5. When did the wear start, and where is it heading? (change point, regression)

Segmented regression leak = a + b * max(0, t - tau), with the change point tau found by least squares over every 0.05 day (t in production days, Monday 06:00 = 0).

| Result | Value |
| --- | --- |
| estimated start of the wear | **Tue 13:30** (t = 1.50) |
| level before | 0.910 sccm |
| slope after (least squares on the rising part) | 0.291 sccm per day [0.236, 0.346], t = 10.5, p < 0.0001 |
| R squared of the rising part | 0.52 |
| mean leak rate reaches the 2.0 sccm limit (half the parts fail) | **next Mon 09:44**, 95 % band Fri 19:16 to next Mon 17:27 |

In the generator the wear starts at the beginning of Wednesday, in daily steps. The change point lands within one shift of it from the data alone; it comes out a little early because the model fits a straight line to what is really a staircase (Tukey in section 4 confirms Tuesday and Wednesday differ, Monday and Tuesday do not). The extrapolation assumes the wear stays linear, which is the weakest assumption in this report: it is a warning of the order of magnitude (days, not weeks), not a schedule.

## 7. Does the shift matter? (chi-square test of independence)

First attempts, shift x result: shift A 2.84 % failures, shift B 1.76 %; chi2 = 1.32, df = 1, p = 0.2507, Cramer's V = 0.036. H0 (independence) is not rejected: no evidence that the shift matters. That is not proof that it doesn't; with so few failures the test could only detect a large difference.

## 8. Are the retests honest? (Welch test, regression to the mean)

10 LEAK-1 parts were retested after failing. Their retest values average 0.977 sccm, against 1.648 sccm for first attempts on Thursday-Friday: difference -0.672 sccm, 95 % CI [-0.856, -0.487], Welch p < 0.0001. The retests read like an unworn seal (Monday-Tuesday mean 0.890).

Some drop is expected even if nothing changed (regression to the mean: parts selected because they read high will read lower next time), but not this much. Re-seating the part temporarily hides the wear, and every rescued part makes the station look healthier than it is. A retest rule should require the second reading to be close to the first, not just inside the limit.

## 9. A process that is in control (Xbar-R chart)

TORQ-2 torque, 20 subgroups of 5 consecutive actuators, limits from Monday-Tuesday (8 subgroups): centre 4.087 Nm, limits 3.943 to 4.230 Nm, R-bar 0.248 Nm. Western Electric signals: 0. The torque process did not change during the week, and the chart correctly stays quiet: a control chart is only useful if it also says nothing when nothing happened.

## 10. Are two measurements related? (correlation)

EOL-2 measures the actuator current and stroke in the same run (n = 99): r = 0.082, 95 % CI [-0.117, 0.275], p = 0.4196. No evidence of a relation, so a high current does not predict a short stroke here and both limits have to be checked separately.
